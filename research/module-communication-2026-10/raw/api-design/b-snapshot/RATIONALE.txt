@get-modular/observation, variant B "watch what you read": rationale for every public name and parameter
=========================================================================================================

Basis for the ADR text. Revised 2026-10-17 after three adversarial reviews (ADVERSARIAL.txt F1-F13, ADVERSARIAL-2.txt
N1-N11, ADVERSARIAL-3.txt M1-M6) and the owner-level decisions Q1-Q3, N1-N10 and M1-M6. Paths are relative to
scratchpad/api-shape/.

Evidence labels
- MEASURED: a compile probe, runtime probe or scenario in this directory; tools/run-all.sh reproduces it.
- WRITERS: the agent-writer rounds (runs/, GRADING.md, the lead's blind results).
- ARCHIVE: a precedent cited, with sources, in the research archive (research/observation-hooks-2026-10/design.md,
  research/module-communication-2026-10/raw/synthesis-*.md), GRADING.md or ADVERSARIAL.txt. Not re-checked online here.
- KNOWLEDGE: my own knowledge of a public API, not re-checked online in this session. Verify before an ADR quotes it.

Model in one paragraph
A store has one writer, its owner. Other modules receive a read-only facet through a contract and a required slot,
typed with their own slice. A module reads a frozen snapshot and follows exactly the keys of that snapshot from its
revision. A watch binds to the subscriber's resources.signal, and nothing is registered in the scope; a load also opens
a child scope for its drain. Delivery is in a later task,
coalesced, to synchronous handlers; asynchronous reactions go through a latest-only load lane. Failures of subscriber
callbacks go to the owner's reportError. A cascade guard bounds chains of commits made by deliveries. Nothing is
global or ambient: no bus, no proxies, no auto-tracking.


1. createStore(initial, options): Store<V>
------------------------------------------
Why: the only way to create observable state; the writer is whoever holds the Store.
Precedent: Redux createStore, Zustand create, Kotlin MutableStateFlow(initial) (KNOWLEDGE).
Rejected: a hub of participants collected through many() (ADR-0034 draft), replaced by subscriptions in the
subscriber's scope (synthesis A 2.1, owner decision).

1.1 initial: V with V extends object & Required<V> & FixedKeys<V>
Why: the key set of the store is the key set of `initial`, fixed at creation.
Without Required: an optional key type-checks but does not exist at run time (sketch v4 defect "Key types").
Without FixedKeys (F9): createStore<Record<string, X>>({}) compiled, and set/read of "session-42" failed at run time
with observation.keys.unknown. FixedKeys rejects every index signature: string, number and template patterns
(`k${number}`), also next to literal keys. Interfaces with literal keys pass.
Evidence: MEASURED, probes/p9-traps.b.ts (TS2344 for Record<string,...>, `session-${number}`, a literal key plus an
index signature) and type-tests ("optional keys are not allowed"), both compilers.
Post-archive correction (2026-10-17): the original check tested the whole key union at once. A required literal key
therefore hid an open template-pattern index signature beside it. FixedKeys now checks each member of keyof V and
rejects any open member, while retaining the existing rejection of an empty key set. The expanded p9 probe rejects
five mixed open-key forms on both compilers; finite literal and template-union records still compile. The original
public types accept all five rejected forms. See ../corrections-2026-10-17/ for the before/after evidence.
Message (N8): the constraint names the reason, "a store record needs literal keys, not an index signature", instead of
"does not satisfy the constraint 'never'" (MEASURED, both compilers).
Rejected: a runtime schema; NoInfer tricks.

1.2 options.name (required): names the store in ListenerError.source and in the entry "store:<name>".
Precedent: every scope and entry of @get-modular/resources has a mandatory name.

1.3 options.resources (required): the store's lifetime is the owner's scope; it closes at its LIFO slot.
Evidence: MEASURED, runtime-b R2, R6. Precedent: ADR-0030; .NET IDisposable, VS Code Disposable (ARCHIVE).

1.4 options.reportError (required)
Why: subscriber callbacks are isolated; their failures, and observation.source.closed, go to one owner-side sink that
the owner wires to the Host's error port.
Evidence: MEASURED, runtime probe 15 and scenarios c, g, i (a negative with `reportError: () => undefined` fails).
Precedent: VS Code Emitter onListenerError (ARCHIVE); isolation against Svelte #11555 and Jotai #2871 (ARCHIVE).
Rejected: a global handler; throwing to the writer; a per-subscriber error callback.
Known limit (ADVERSARIAL section 3): with plugins, a Host that follows a plugin's store sends its own handler failures
to the plugin's reportError. Acceptable for trusted in-process code; name it in the ADR.

1.5 options.maxSubscriptionsPerScope (default 64) and options.maxSubscriptions (default 8192)
Why (owner decision Q1): a leak must fail in the scope that leaks, not in an innocent module's factory, and dynamic
instances must scale. The per-scope limit counts live subscriptions of one store in one subscriber scope; the
per-store limit is a safety net the owner sets. Both throw observation.subscriptions.limit; the message names the
scope (by path once Resources.path exists).
Without it (F4): with one per-store limit of 1024, one leaking module made the next innocent factory throw, and
200 sessions x 6 modules (1200 subscriptions) failed only under load.
Evidence: MEASURED, runtime probe 8 and runtime-b R5 (64 accepted in the leaking scope, the healthy scope still
subscribes), runtime probe 10 (per-store safety across three scopes stops at 100 when set to 100).
Message (N7): a leak through child scopes that are never closed passes the per-scope limit (one subscription each) and
reaches the per-store limit; the first refused subscription is the leaking module's own, but later ones may be an
innocent module's. The per-store message therefore states the number of scopes and the largest count in one ("in 3
scopes, at most 1 in one", MEASURED, runtime probe 26) and names the pattern; once Resources.path exists it lists the
three largest holders by path.
Precedent: Get Modular's 1024 providers per binding row (synthesis A 2.1); VS Code leakWarningThreshold (ARCHIVE);
Node EventEmitter defaultMaxListeners = 10 per emitter and event, warning only (KNOWLEDGE).
Recipes against the limit: a child scope per piece of work, or one subscription that fans out (README "Recipes").
Rejected: one per-store limit (F4); warnings only.


2. Store<in out V> (the owner's facet): read, set, readonly, close
------------------------------------------------------------------
in out V: set consumes V and read produces it; a Store<Wide> is never a Store<Slice>.

2.1 read: as 3.1.

2.2 set<const K extends Key<V>>(patch: { readonly [P in K]: Frozen<V[P]> }): number
Why: commits a change of some keys and returns the revision; other keys keep their values.
- The mapped form infers K from the patch, so undefined for a string key is rejected even without
  exactOptionalPropertyTypes (MEASURED, probes/p7), and unknown keys and wrong types are rejected.
- Frozen<V[P]> accepts both a fresh mutable value and a frozen value read back from a snapshot (MEASURED, p9).
- A conditional spread `set({ ...(x ? { theme: x } : {}) })` does not compile; the recipe is `if (x !== undefined)
  { store.set({ theme: x }); }` (MEASURED, p9; README "Small things").
Ownership (F7, owner decision): set and createStore take ownership of the objects and freeze them in place; nodes
frozen before are not walked again (a WeakSet). They validate every value first and freeze only after every check
passed (N9): a failing set (not-plain value, unknown key, echo, cascade) leaves the caller's objects unfrozen
(MEASURED, runtime probe 26). README: copy objects that belong to another module before passing them. MEASURED, runtime probe 21: appending one row to 50 000 rows costs
about 7-9 ms per set, against 16 ms when frozen nodes are walked again (mutation rewalk-frozen) and 20 ms in the
review's measurement of the previous stub. The remaining cost is the walk of the new 50 000-slot array itself, linear
in the size of each new container. A caller's object is frozen after set (MEASURED, probe 21).
Rejected: copying every value (doubles memory for large values); walking the whole value on every set.
Cascade guard (F2): a commit made by a delivery callback is one cascade step deeper; see section 5.

2.3 readonly(): ReadonlyStore<V>
Why: the facet other modules receive; no set, no close.
Evidence: MEASURED, probes/p8 (`const leaked: ReadonlyStore<App> = store` fails, TS2741, because the facet carries the
type marker that Store does not have; this holds even if Store ever gains watch and load) and p5 (GM contracts).
Precedent: Svelte readonly(store), Kotlin asStateFlow(), Angular asReadonly() (KNOWLEDGE).
Rejected: readonly(keys) runtime projection (deferred, additive; the marker types it as ReadonlyStore<Pick<V, K>>).

2.4 close(): undefined (owner decision Q3, revised by N4 and N5)
Why: closes the store mid-life (the owner's scope also closes it at the LIFO slot).
Semantics: every live subscription is detached (a watch's signal listener is removed; a load's lane scope load:<name>
is closed), so nothing stays in a long-lived subscriber scope; each subscription gets one report with
observation.source.closed; running loads are aborted. Then set, watch, load and the facet's read throw
observation.store.closed, while the owner's own read keeps returning the last values (N4).
Why the owner keeps read (N4): an owner that opens a file first, creates the store from it, and saves the final state
in the file's cleanup runs that cleanup after the store closed (LIFO). With read throwing, the last change was lost
(review C11: debt store.closed, saved ""). MEASURED now: runtime-b R11 saves "dark"; runtime probe 25 shows the
recommended form too (a save entry registered after createStore runs before the store closes).
Why a watch is bound to resources.signal only (N5, then M3): with one scope entry per watch, a long-lived subscriber
scope kept about 511 bytes per closed source until it closed itself (a session dashboard over a million sessions keeps
about half a gigabyte). A child scope per watch removed that but cost three times the memory of a live watch. Nothing
needs to be registered at all: resources aborts the signal synchronously before any cleanup (#request in #close and
#join, VERIFIED in origin/main scope.ts), so the abort listener detaches the watch first; an entry or a child added no
debt and only retention. MEASURED (runtime-b/measure-watch-memory.ts, results/runtime-b.watch-memory.txt):
  signal binding only: 1064 B per live watch, 22 B kept per closed source (noise);
  child scope per watch: 3080 B and -93 B; one entry per watch: 1282 B and 511 B.
The signal binding is the default; at the limits (8192 per store) the worst case is about 9 MB per store.
Without it (F6): 50 of 50 closed stores and their handlers stayed reachable from a long-lived subscriber scope, the
subscriber was not told, and read after close silently returned old values.
Evidence: MEASURED, runtime probe 4 and runtime-b R2 (the subscriber scope stays open and referenced; the handler is
collected; the control with an open store keeps it); the mutation retain-after-close is caught.
Rejected (Q3): a required onSourceClosed handler in every watch (boilerplate nearly always unused); deferring.


3. ReadonlyStore<out V> (the facet): [storeValue], read, watch, load
-------------------------------------------------------------------
3.0 The type marker readonly [storeValue]: V (type-only unique symbol; no such property at run time)
Why (F1): before the marker, a slice was protected only when a ReadonlyStore<A> was compared with a ReadonlyStore<B>
alias to alias. Readonly<>, Pick<…, "read">, a hand-written port of member types, or a member type alone accepted a
store of a different value type on both compilers, and CMS teaches writing one's own application port.
Evidence: MEASURED, probes/p8-structural.b.ts: Readonly<>, Pick<"read">, intersections, hand-written read+watch ports,
member type read, Promise and arrays of a wrong slice are now rejected on tsc7 and 5.8.3; probes/p10-gm-main-implement
on get-modular origin/main sources (implement<C>()(declaration, factory, scoped)): Pick<ReadonlyStore<WrongSlice>,
"read"> is now rejected.
Residual (documented in p8): a port made of `watch` alone (Pick<…, "watch"> or the member type) still compiles; it has
no snapshots of its own, so it cannot follow anything. README rule: type a dependency only as ReadonlyStore<Slice>.
Side effect (accepted): a snapshot read through a slice facet cannot be passed to the wide facet's watch (p8).
The marker is also on Snapshot and SnapshotOf, so every structural comparison sees V.

3.1 read<const K extends Keys<V>>(keys: K): Snapshot<V, K[number]>
Why: the current values of exactly the keys a module needs; the explicit dependency list. After close it throws
observation.store.closed (Q3). Precedent: React useSyncExternalStore getSnapshot; Kubernetes list (ARCHIVE).

3.2 Snapshot<V, K> = { revision, values: Frozen<Pick<V, K>>, [brand], [storeValue] }
- revision: commit number of the latest commit that changed one of the keys (Kubernetes resourceVersion, etcd
  revision, ARCHIVE). Not proof that a value changed.
- values: deeply read-only at type level (F9): lists.read(["ids"]).values.ids.push(2) no longer compiles (MEASURED,
  p9); at run time the values are frozen. Frozen keeps primitives as they are, so branded IDs (`string & { __brand }`)
  survive, and maps tuples element by element (N1; MEASURED, p9: loadUser(snap.values.user) and
  `const first: string = snap.values.pair[0]` compile). A domain type that receives store values is declared readonly;
  a mutable array type does not accept a frozen value (MEASURED, p9, intended).
- brand: a hand-built snapshot does not compile; a copy or a snapshot of another store with the same type is rejected
  at run time with observation.snapshot.foreign (WeakMap origin; MEASURED, runtime probe B, probes/p6).

3.3 watch<S extends SnapshotOf<V>>(snapshot: S, spec: { resources, name, onChange }): undefined
Why: the keys and the start revision come from what the module read: keys are written once and the "read, then
subscribe" race is closed by construction. No `since` concept.
Evidence: MEASURED, runtime probe 1, scenario e-async-factory (a commit during a real await is delivered); WRITERS,
round 2 and 3 blind runs (B: larger model 9/9 as recorded, smaller model 9/9 after gate calibration). Archive note
(2026-10-17): the later, stricter gate of F11 finds that the round 2 smaller-model run and the second round 3
smaller-model run report only the first screen failure in task h.
Precedent: Kubernetes list+watch from resourceVersion, etcd watch from start_revision, React useSyncExternalStore,
Vue watch(source, callback, options) (ARCHIVE, lead's brief).
Rejected: A (keys + changesAfter; kept as the documented alternative); C (atomic watch returning the snapshot: lost
update across one await, GRADING); a union watch spec (misleading errors, probes/standalone/p2).

3.3.1 spec.resources (required)
A watch is bound to this scope's signal (M3) and detached synchronously when the scope starts closing, before any
cleanup or drain; a load also opens a child scope load:<name> for its drain. If resources.signal is already aborted, watch and load call
signal.throwIfAborted() (F10): resources.scope.closed when the scope is closing, or the Host's own reason when the
constructing run was cancelled. No observation-specific code; the module lets the factory fail.
Evidence: MEASURED, runtime-b R1, R8 (both cases, the second through scoped() from origin/main).
Layer (F12, clarified by the implementation brief): watch and load are called in the module's composition file; domain and application code never see
Resources (CMS rule 11). CMS rule 5 must change in the same delivery: see CMS-RULE5-DRAFT.txt.
Precedent: addEventListener(type, listener, { signal }); AbortSignal.throwIfAborted (web platform, KNOWLEDGE);
Angular DestroyRef, VS Code Disposable, .NET IOptionsMonitor.OnChange (ARCHIVE).
Rejected: a returned unsubscribe handle; an optional signal; a separate observation.scope.closing code (two codes in
two packages for one state, F10).

3.3.2 spec.name (required, unique per store within one scope)
Why: attribution (ListenerError.subscription) and the guard against build-up: a second live subscription with the
same name on one store in one scope throws observation.subscription.duplicate-name (renamed from
watch.duplicate-name, F8, so that it fits load and later subscribe). Convention "<module>/<purpose>".
Evidence: MEASURED, runtime probe 8, runtime-b R5; WRITERS round 1 (optional names in C were omitted 3/3 and collided).

3.3.3 spec.onChange: (next: S) => undefined
Synchronous, idempotent, in a later task, coalesced. `=> undefined` rejects async handlers, expression bodies that
return a value (void included) and named handlers typed `: void`; a named handler is annotated `: undefined`
(MEASURED, p9, F13). After a throw the same subscription keeps receiving later revisions. A handler never changes a
key its subscription follows, neither with set nor through a command, also asynchronously (vision rule 7; guarded by
observation.delivery.echo, section 5). Writing the module's own, different store is the derived-store recipe and is
fine. `watch` is written on concrete types; a generic helper over ReadonlyStore<V> cannot follow what it read (TS2345,
the constraint form of 3.3; unchanged since round 1).

3.4 load<S extends SnapshotOf<V>, T>(snapshot: S, spec): undefined
Latest-only lane: one active run, at most one pending; a change aborts the running run; results are delivered only if
no commit has touched the snapshot's keys since (store revision).
Evidence: MEASURED, runtime probes 6, 17, runtime-b R3, R7, R10; mutations queue-loads, compare-last-offered,
no-loading-signal caught. Precedent: LaunchDarkly js-core #840/#842 (ARCHIVE); Angular resource status "loading",
TanStack Query pending (GRADING, quoted).
3.4.1 resources, name: as 3.3.1, 3.3.2. A load opens resources.child({ name: "load:<name>" }) with one setup entry
"drain" whose cleanup receives escalate; close semantics MEASURED in runtime-b R6 (no debts for a cooperative loader;
failed debt observation.load.still-running after escalation; pending debt after abandon).
3.4.2 loader (signal-aware, returns a Promise); name as in Angular resource({ loader }) (KNOWLEDGE).
3.4.3 onLoading (required): called when a run is queued, before the superseded run settles (GRADING F1/F2). It closes
the gap from the delivery of a change on: no extra region check at call time is needed (round 4 writers asked). In the
synchronous turn of the commit itself every subscriber still shows the previous state; closing that too would need
synchronous delivery inside set, which the model rejects (re-entrancy into the writer).
3.4.4 onResult and onError (required): delivered only for values still current in the store (GRADING F4).


4. ListenerError = { source, subscription, path?, revision, cause, [brand] } (owner side only)
---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Why public: it is the parameter type of reportError and of the Host's error port types.
Why branded: only the store creates reports; a subscriber lets its handler throw. In round 2 both smaller-model writers built
fake reports by hand (runs/r2-*-small-1/i-errors.ts); that is now a compile error (type-tests, TS2741/TS2345).
Renamed (F8): `store` became `source`, so that events can report through the same type later.
path (F8): the subscriber's scope path, present once @get-modular/resources provides Resources.path. DEPENDENCY: an
additive, read-only Resources.path in resources 0.2 (owner of that package to confirm). The stub already includes a
path when the Resources object has one.


5. Echo and cascade guards (F2, N2, N3, M1, M2; vision rule 7: "a runaway cascade fails with a coded error")
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Delivery context: onChange, onLoading, onResult, onError and the loader body run in a context { depth, origin
subscription, live }. It is carried synchronously everywhere, and through await and microtasks where AsyncLocalStorage
exists, reached without a static import through globalThis.process?.getBuiltinModule?.("node:async_hooks") (Node 22.3
and later; the supported Node 24 and 26 have it; Bun 1.3.11 too, measured by the reviewer).
Bounded lifetime (M1, P0 in the third review): AsyncLocalStorage captures the context when an async resource is
created, so a timer, server or connection created inside a handler inherited it forever. MEASURED by the reviewer on
the unbounded version: a heartbeat started by the owner from a command crashed the process with a false echo; 69 of 70
admin requests to a server restarted by a handler failed; independent requests accumulated depth until the cascade
guard broke a derived store after 32 requests. Now a context counts only while it is live: a handler's context until
the end of its delivery task, a loader's context until its returned Promise settles. One live flag per delivery task; it is closed
by a second immediate queued right behind the flush when the flush is scheduled, so it closes after the flush's
microtask checkpoint and before any timer or I/O callback of the next loop iteration. (An immediate queued from inside
the flush would run only in the next iteration, after its timers and I/O.)
Post-archive correction (2026-10-17): onResult and onError now get their own scheduled delivery task and live flag,
separate from the loader window. The original shared flag closed before their microtasks ran, allowing repeated
echoes and resetting cascade depth. Currentness is checked again at delivery, because a commit or close may happen
after the Promise settled. Queued publication is counted for delivered() through its complete callback task,
including nested microtasks. Loader drain tracks only actual loader work; a canceled queued callback cannot become
an observation.load.still-running debt. Evidence: runtime-b/review-regressions.test.ts R16 and R17; the original engine
fails all six callback microtask/await echo cases and the callback-to-watch cross-store cascade case.
The existing runtime probe 17 and runtime-b R7 now also commit after publication is queued, before the callback task.
The earlier commit-before-loader-settlement ordering alone no longer distinguished compare-last-offered once
publication moved to a later task. Both strengthened probes reject that existing mutant; no mutation was removed.
Echo (N3): a commit made in a live context that changes a key the origin subscription follows on the same store fails
at once with observation.delivery.echo.
Cascade (N2): every other commit made in a live context is one step deeper than its delivery; a delivery takes the
largest depth among the commits it coalesces; a commit deeper than 64 fails with observation.delivery.cascade.
Report and throw, fail fast (M2): a failing commit changes nothing, is reported to the reportError of the store it was
made to, and then throws. In a fire-and-forget async continuation the throw is an unhandled rejection, which Node's
default policy turns into a process exit. That is intended for a programming bug (the alternative is a silent endless
loop); the README says so, and a Host may choose its own unhandled-rejection policy. A synchronous handler's invoke does
not report the same error a second time.
Evidence: MEASURED.
- Echo at the first round: runtime probe 18 (C3, synchronous), 22 (C1 async command, C2 queueMicrotask, C10 loader
  body), runtime-b R9 and R13 (C4: a load whose onResult advances the cursor it follows).
- No false reports from work that outlives the delivery (runtime-b R13): heartbeat (the owner's interval writes the
  watched key: no report, no crash), server (30 admin requests through a server restarted by a handler: all ok),
  depth (40 independent requests: no cascade report; the derived key equals the hits). The mutation unbounded-context
  makes all three fail.
- Cross-store asynchronous ping-pong through commands stops at 65 deliveries with one cascade report: probe 23, R9.
- Synchronous ping-pong and a legitimate chain of 20 derived stores: probe 19. Pagination as a loop inside one loader
  passes 100 pages: probe 24.
- Without AsyncLocalStorage (mutation no-async-context), synchronous detection remains (probes 18, 19).
  Post-archive correction (2026-10-17): the loader invocation itself enters the synchronous context after the engine's
  startup trampoline. The extended env-probe no-node case rejects a top-of-loader echo on the first run; the original
  engine invokes the loader five times before the probe's safety cap.
Not caught (known item, section 13): an echo that crosses a timer or real I/O (a command that awaits a network call
before it writes). Such a loop runs at the speed of that I/O and does not end by itself. Environments without
getBuiltinModule (browsers, some Jest environments) catch synchronous chains only; tests there miss what production on
Node throws, which is why /testing exports asyncDetection for a test preset to assert.
Bound: 64 steps. Work started outside any live context (timers, input events, I/O) starts at depth 0.
Loader window (L1): work a loader starts (a timer, a connection, a server) runs in the loader's context while the run
is active, so its write to a key that load follows counts as an echo (the reviewer measured a false echo from an owner's
heartbeat started by a command the loader called). Inside AsyncLocalStorage the loader's own continuation and a timer it
created cannot be told apart, so the rule is documented instead: a loader does not start long-lived work (README
checklist, LoadSpec.loader JSDoc).
"A later task" (L2, L3): resolved once at module load: setImmediate from node:timers through
process.getBuiltinModule where it exists, else a global setImmediate, else setTimeout(callback, 0). Importing never
throws a ReferenceError (browsers, renderers), and a fake global setImmediate installed before the package loads cannot
stall deliveries or the testing deadline. MEASURED, runtime-b/env-probe.ts: "fake-before" (a never-firing global
setImmediate installed before import) delivers and delivered() resolves; "no-node" (no global setImmediate, no
getBuiltinModule) imports, delivers through setTimeout, reports asyncDetection false and still catches a synchronous
watch echo at the first round. Its post-archive extension also checks the synchronous loader body. With the global
lookup only (mutation global-timers-only) "fake-before" hangs.
Supported environments: Node 24 and 26 fully (asynchronous detection, setImmediate ordering); Bun verified by the
reviewer (getBuiltinModule and AsyncLocalStorage propagation present). Elsewhere: synchronous detection only, and no
ordering guarantee beyond "a later task".


6. The testing entry point (F3, N6, M4; vision rule 16)
------------------------------------------------------
@get-modular/observation/testing: liveSubscriptions(facet, resources?), delivered(options?), settled(options?),
asyncDetection. Options: { stores?, signal?, timeoutMs? }.
Deadline configured by default (M4): timeoutMs defaults to 5000 ms of real time, Infinity only when passed; a signal (for example
node:test t.signal) ends a wait earlier and never switches the deadline off. Both give observation.testing.timeout.
The reviewer measured that a never-aborting t.signal and a mocked setImmediate each made the old wait hang forever.
Captured timers (M4): in Node the package captures setImmediate from node:timers and performance.now from
node:perf_hooks through process.getBuiltinModule, for deliveries and for the waits. With those builtins unmodified,
replacement global timers or a replacement globalThis.performance object installed before or after import stall
neither. MEASURED, runtime-b R14: a never-aborting signal with
timeoutMs 200 gives the code; with node:test mock.timers on setImmediate, setTimeout and Date, a delivery still runs and
settled() still reaches its deadline. Patching performance.now on Node's shared performance object also changes
the builtin clock, so that mock must be installed after import. The same import order applies to other builtin
mocks, including node:test mock.timers for node:timers. Without the builtins the package captures the available globals;
that fallback cannot make previously faked globals real.
Post-archive correction (2026-10-17): the original /testing captured an already-faked global performance.now and never
expired its deadline. env-probe fake-clock-before freezes global performance.now and Date.now before import: a blocked
load still gets observation.testing.timeout at 20 ms, before the independent 500 ms watchdog. The original engine
reaches the watchdog instead. The preserved before/after evidence is in ../corrections-2026-10-17/.
Why the store filter (N6): a global wait never finished while any other store committed every task (C5) or another
module held a long-poll load open (C6). MEASURED, runtime-b R12.
asyncDetection (M6): true where asynchronous echoes are detected; a test preset asserts it (R14).
Leak check: assert the store-wide liveSubscriptions(facet) === 0 after closing the module (C9).
Import rule draft: /testing only from test files, enforced by one lint line in the CMS preset (CMS-RULE5-DRAFT.txt).
Packaging: this is a second export; ADR-0034 spoke of one root export. The lead handles that in the ADR.


7. Slices through contracts (owner decision Q2)
-----------------------------------------------
The owner publishes a few area contracts (theme, logging, region) and hands out the same facet under each; a consumer
slots its area contract and narrows further in its own port type ReadonlyStore<Slice>, protected by the marker (3.0).
Why: a revision of one area contract changes only that area's consumers; no central list of consumer contracts at the
owner (vision rule 11); plugins receive the Host's area contracts.
Rejected (F5): one wide ReadonlyStore<AppSettings> contract (every new key is a lockstep revision for every consumer);
a consumer-owned slice contract that the owner provides (central list, inverted dependency, unavailable to plugins).
Evidence: MEASURED, probes/p10 (narrowing through implement on GM main), probes/p5 (contracts on GM 0.4.0).


8. Errors: ObservationError with code
-------------------------------------
Compare `code`, never class or message. Thrown: argument.invalid, keys.empty, keys.unknown, values.not-plain,

9. Limits and costs (MEASURED; timings informative, not assertions)
-------------------------------------------------------------------
- Coalescing: one buffered entry per subscription (10 000 sets in one task -> one delivery each). Probe 5, R3.
- At most one delivery per task per subscription. R3.
- Load lane: one active run, at most one pending. Probe 6, R3.
- Dispatch: 500 subscriptions on 500 keys, a set of one key: 1 index lookup, 1 visit, 1 callback, about 0.36 us. R4.
- Limits: 64 per subscriber scope, 8192 per store (1.5). Echo: first round; cascade: 64 steps (5).
- Heap: about 1.1 KB per live watch, about 0 B kept per closed source (2.4).
- After close (subscriber scope or store), no reference to subscriber callbacks remains. Probe 3, 4, R2.
- Large values: section 2.2.
- Probe sensitivity: deliberate defects in the stub engine are each caught: 15 by runtime/probes, 16 by runtime-b
  (unbounded-context only there) (tools/mutations*.sh, with a 90 s watchdog for defects that would hang).


10. TypeScript variance: the risk, the CI probes and the fallback
-----------------------------------------------------------------
Now: the type marker [storeValue]: V makes every structural comparison check V. `out V` stays as a confirmation; it
is no longer load-bearing: the control without the annotation (controls/b-no-variance-annotation) passes every
expectation of probes/p3 and p8 on tsc7 and 5.8.3. The earlier risk (constraints not compared in variance checks)
matters only for the compile-time rejection of a foreign snapshot shape in watch; the marker covers the slices.
Note: tsc7 7.0.2 accepts R1<App> as R1<Wrong> for an alias without annotation or marker, where TS 5.8.3 rejects it
(probes/standalone-sig/tsc7-variance-repro.ts, from the review; MEASURED again here). It looks like a tsc7 bug; whether
to report it upstream is the owner's decision. With the marker it no longer affects this API.
CI probes (both compilers): b-snapshot/type-tests.ts (--expect), probes/p8-structural.b.ts, probes/p9-traps.b.ts,
probes/p5-gm-contracts.b.ts (GM 0.4.0), probes/p10-gm-main-implement.b.ts (--gm-main, origin/main sources),
controls/b-v-in-param (must keep failing, TS2636), controls/b-no-variance-annotation (must keep passing: the marker
carries the check). A canary on the next tsc7 release is advisable.
Fallback if a compiler rejects the constraint form: watch<P>(snapshot: Snapshot<P>, …) with the marker kept; a foreign
snapshot shape is then rejected at run time only.


11. Deliberately not in the API
-------------------------------
Events (later; they will reuse the cascade guard and ListenerError.source), selectors and per-subscriber equality, a
computed graph, a source status, history or replay, interop adapters, a readonly(keys) projection, an updater form of
set, a free-function watch over several stores (the review found a tuple form workable but it turns a snapshot into a
capability; the method form keeps the slot as the only capability).


12. Open points
---------------
- Resources.path is a dependency on @get-modular/resources 0.2 (additive); until then reports carry no path.
- CMS rule 5 must be rewritten in the same delivery (CMS-RULE5-DRAFT.txt); resources' own wording for signal ("stops
  acquisition, never closes anything") should mention that observation detaches subscriptions on it.
- Supported environments: section 5 and section 13 (Node 24 and 26 fully, Bun verified, elsewhere synchronous detection
  only).
- /testing is a second package export; ADR-0034 said one root export (the lead handles it in the ADR).
- Abort-listener isolation (a throwing abort listener becomes an uncaught exception in Node) is still undecided.
- One-task lag of load state after a commit; a synchronous status handle would remove it.
- The cascade bound of 64 and the limits 64/8192 are judgement calls, not measured requirements.
- An echo is defined on keys of the same store. A module that follows key a and, from its handler, writes key b of
  the same store that another subscription of its own follows is not an echo; it is bounded by the cascade depth.


13. ADR known items (owner and trigger for each)
------------------------------------------------
- An echo that crosses a timer or real I/O is not caught (the price of M1's bounded context). Owner: observation.
  Trigger: AsyncContext (TC39) available in the supported runtimes, or the first incident of such a loop.
- Holders by path in the subscriptions.limit message (N7) and ListenerError.path (F8). Owner: resources (an additive,
  read-only Resources.path). Trigger: Resources.path in @get-modular/resources 0.2.
- Supported environments: Node 24 and 26 fully; Bun verified. Elsewhere (browsers, Electron renderers without Node
  integration, some Jest environments) the package imports and works with synchronous detection only and no ordering
  guarantee beyond "a later task" (setImmediate, else setTimeout(0)). Owner: ADR author with observation. Trigger: the
  first consumer outside Node, or a test runner where asyncDetection is false.
- The loader window (L1): long-lived work started from a loader runs in its context while the run is active, so its
  write to a key the load follows is reported as an echo. Documented as a rule, not prevented. Owner: observation.
  Trigger: implementation of the load lane in the package, or the first such report.
- A second package export (/testing) against ADR-0034's one root export. Owner: the lead, in the ADR. Trigger: ADR text.
- CMS rules 5 and 11 and the /testing import rule (CMS-RULE5-DRAFT.txt) land in common-assembly.md and the CMS preset in
  the same delivery as the package. Owner: Get Modular standard. Trigger: the package delivery.
- Abort-listener isolation (a throwing listener on a native AbortSignal becomes an uncaught exception in Node).
  Owner: observation. Trigger: implementation of the load lane in the package.
- The bounds (64 cascade steps, 64 per scope, 8192 per store) are judgement calls. Owner: observation. Trigger: a real
  consumer that hits one of them legitimately.
- M3 (watch bound to the signal only) is done in this revision, not deferred.
